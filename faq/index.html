Getting around

Where do I start?

Tap any mountain on the map or in the list. You get one line on how the week looks, then a strip of days. Tap a day to see why it got its grade and what the lifts will feel like hour by hour from 8am to 4pm.

How do I keep my mountains at the top?

Tap the star next to any mountain. Starred mountains show under Mine in the resort list and in the weekend planner. Stars are saved on this device only; there's no account.

How does the weekend planner choose?

It grades Saturday and Sunday at every open mountain (or just your starred mountains, or one state) and names the single best day and mountain. It also tells you if one day is clearly better across most mountains, for example when rain moves in on Sunday. The boxed day in each row is the better day at that mountain.

Where are the charts and the raw model numbers?

On any mountain, open Forecast details. You'll find hour-by-hour snow, precipitation type and temperature charts, lift wind for every day, every model's numbers, and the odds of each snow total.

Can I pick which models show on the chart?

Yes. Tap a model's name above the snow chart to hide or show it, or use the quick sets: Short-range (the high-resolution models that see two days out), Global (the big models that see a week out) or Blend only. Move your finger or mouse across the chart to read the snow, temperatures and wind for that hour. Your choice is remembered on this device.

Can I share a mountain?

Every mountain has its own page, like Killington. Copy the address from your browser and send it.

Accuracy

How accurate is it?

Every day we check what each model said one to three days earlier against what airport weather stations near the resorts actually measured. The results are on the model scoreboard. Upslope gives more weight to the models doing best in each part of the mountains, so the blend should beat any single model over time.

As a rule of thumb, the next two days are usually close, three to five days out is a good guide to whether a storm is coming, and six to seven days out is a heads-up only.

What does “models 6–14″” mean?

It's the lowest and highest total among the eight weather models. A narrow range means the models agree. A wide range means the storm track or the snow-rain line is still uncertain, so plan for the middle and watch for updates.

What does “70% chance of 6″ or more” mean?

Two of the models, the American GFS and the European, are each rerun dozens of times with slightly different starting weather. Upslope counts how many of those 82 runs reach each total. If 57 of 82 runs reach 6″, that's about a 70% chance.

What is the trend line?

It compares this forecast with the one from about a day ago over the same days. “Trending up: 6″ yesterday, 9″ now” means the models have been adding snow, which often keeps happening as a storm gets closer.

Why does it differ from my resort's snow report?

Upslope forecasts the summit and the base separately, and resort reports usually measure at one spot mid-mountain. Wind also moves snow around: exposed summits can measure less and sheltered glades much more. Treat our numbers as what falls, not what you'll find in every trail.

Reading the numbers

What do the day grades mean?

Each day gets an A to F rating for skiing. A: fresh snow or a sunny, calm day on good snow. C: a normal groomer day. F: rain, or lifts likely stopped by wind. Grades only show once a mountain is usually open.

What's a powder morning?

Snow that falls between 4pm and 9am, so it's waiting on the first chair. The score goes up with more overnight snow, lighter snow and calmer wind, and down if rain or wind gets in the way.

What does "snow feel" mean?

How light or heavy the new snow will be. In cold air an inch of water makes 15 or more inches of light, fluffy snow. Near freezing it makes heavy, wet snow, under 8 inches. Wind breaks flakes up and packs them down too.

Why does a day say icy or firm?

When rain or a thaw is followed by a freeze, the surface sets up hard. Upslope flags those mornings and says whether they should soften by midday.

How do you know when lifts might stop for wind?

Each mountain has a rough gust level where upper lifts often close, lower when the wind blows from the direction it's most exposed to. The lift wind grid shows every hour from 8am to 4pm in green, amber or red.

How do you tell snow from sleet and rain?

We use the humidity-adjusted temperature at the summit and the base, plus the temperature about a mile up. A warm layer up there melts snowflakes, which refreeze into sleet or freezing rain on the way down even when it's below freezing on the hill.

Before the season

Why are there no grades yet?

Before a mountain's usual opening day there's nothing to grade, so Upslope tracks snowmaking windows and the first natural snow instead. Opening dates are typical ones, not announcements.

What's a snowmaking window?

Hours when the summit's snowmaking temperature (the air temperature adjusted for humidity) is 27°F or colder. Guns run best below about 22°F. Dry air is why resorts can make snow when the thermometer reads above freezing.

Data and updates

How often does it update?

About every six hours, shortly after each new round of model runs. The time of the last update is at the top of the home page.

Where does the data come from?

Model forecasts from NOAA (HRRR, NAM, GFS and the National Weather Service Blend), ECMWF, Environment Canada (HRDPS and GEM) and Germany's DWD (ICON), served by Open-Meteo. Measured precipitation for the scoreboard comes from airport weather stations via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet.

Is it free?

Yes. Email alerts for your mountains are on the way.

Upslope is free and built by skiers. Forecasts can be wrong; check resort reports and avalanche advisories before you go.

How it works

Built for skiers, not meteorologists.

Five models, one honest range

Most apps show one computer model. We pull five (American GFS and HRRR, European ECMWF, Canadian, German ICON and the National Weather Service blend) and show the middle forecast plus the lowest and highest.

Snow quality

Cold air makes fluffy snow; warm air makes heavy cement. We size up snow totals using the temperature at each summit instead of a fixed ratio, then tell you whether to expect blower powder or a dense base builder.

Powder score

A 0 to 100 score for each morning. It rewards snow that falls overnight before first chair, cold temperatures, no rain, and models that agree with each other.

Rain, wind and snowmaking

East Coast storms often mix with rain and bring big wind. We flag rain days, gusts strong enough to close upper lifts, and cold stretches that are good for snowmaking.

Glossary
Range
The lowest and highest total among the weather models. A wide range means the forecast is uncertain.
Snow level
The elevation where rain turns to snow, roughly 1,000 ft below the freezing level.
Overnight snow
Snow that falls from 4pm to 9am, so it's waiting for you on the first chair.
Wind hold
When gusts get too strong (often above 45 mph) resorts stop upper lifts for safety.
Snowmaking weather
Several hours at or below about 26°F, cold enough for snow guns to work well.

East Coast Snow is free and built by skiers. Forecasts can be wrong; check resort reports and avalanche advisories before you go.

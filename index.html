Method

Built for skiers
01

Five models, one range

Most apps show one computer model. We pull five (American GFS and HRRR, European ECMWF, Canadian, German ICON and the National Weather Service blend) and show the middle forecast with the lowest and highest.

02

Snow quality

Cold air makes light snow; warm air makes heavy snow. Totals are sized using the temperature at each summit instead of a fixed ratio, so you know whether to expect powder or a dense base builder.

03

Powder score

A 0–100 score for each morning. It rewards snow that falls overnight before first chair, cold temperatures, no rain, and models that agree with each other.

04

Rain, wind, snowmaking

East Coast storms often mix with rain and bring strong wind. We flag rain days, gusts strong enough to close upper lifts, and cold stretches that are good for snowmaking.

Glossary
Range
The lowest and highest total among the weather models. A wide range means the forecast is uncertain.
SLR
Snow-to-liquid ratio: inches of snow per inch of water. 8:1 is heavy and wet, 15:1 and up is light powder.
Snow level
The elevation where rain turns to snow, roughly 1,000 ft below the freezing level.
Overnight snow
Snow that falls from 4pm to 9am, so it's waiting for you on the first chair.
Wind hold
When gusts get too strong (often above 45 mph) resorts stop upper lifts for safety.
Snowmaking weather
Several hours at or below about 26°F, cold enough for snow guns to work well.
Upslope snow
Snow squeezed out as moist air is forced up the mountains. It's why the northern Greens and Adirondacks often beat the forecast.

Upslope is free and built by skiers. Forecasts can be wrong; check resort reports and avalanche advisories before you go.
